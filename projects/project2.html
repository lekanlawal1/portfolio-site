AI Support Ticket Triage: Sorting Support Tickets, and Catching the AI When It's Wrong

A tool that reads incoming support tickets and decides what each one is about, how urgent it is and which team should get it. When the AI isn't sure, a person decides instead.

PythonGemini APIstructured output Streamlitprompt engineering

What it does

Support teams spend a few minutes on every ticket just working out what it is and who should handle it. This tool does that first step with Google's Gemini: it labels each ticket with a category, a priority and a team, and gives a confidence score. Anything under 75% confident isn't sent anywhere automatically. It goes to a review pile for a person, with the AI's guess attached as a suggestion.

The rest of the work is making it dependable. Every answer has to come back in a fixed format, so there's no guessing at free text. If the AI service is slow, refuses, or sends something broken, the tool waits and tries again, and if it still fails, the ticket goes to the review pile with the reason written down. Nothing is ever dropped silently.

What testing found

I tested it on 171 tickets with known answers, including 15 written to be ambiguous. The most useful result wasn't the score. Complaints that mentioned money were being filed as billing questions with 95% confidence, because of one tie-breaking rule in my instructions to the AI. The confidence check couldn't catch it, because the AI wasn't unsure, it was consistently wrong. The lesson: a confidence score catches vague tickets, not a systematic mistake, so the mistakes themselves have to be measured.

Why it's archived

The test tickets were ones I wrote, so the 90% can't be checked against real support traffic. Its successor, App Review Insights, keeps the same idea (the AI suggests, code checks) and applies it to real App Store reviews anyone can look up.
