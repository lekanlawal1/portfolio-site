FIFA Player Churn: Predicting Which Players Leave Their Club

Football clubs lose players the way subscription businesses lose customers. Using two editions of the FIFA game's player data, this predicts who is likely to leave their club next season.

DuckDBversioned SQLwindow functions scikit-learnPlotly.js

What it does

Each edition of the FIFA video game lists about 18,000 real players with their club, ratings, wage and position. Comparing two editions shows who moved. I built a pipeline that takes the raw files, cleans them, and trains a model to predict which players will leave, then a dashboard that shows every player's predicted risk next to what actually happened.

The data problem that mattered

Clubs sometimes change their names between editions ("Real Madrid CF" became "Real Madrid"). Taken at face value, that looks like a whole squad leaving at once. A matching step written in SQL found 92 renamed clubs, and fixing them removed 8.4 percentage points of churn that never happened.

0.69 is a modest score, and I say so. The data is missing the thing that matters most: when each player's contract ends. Claiming more would be a warning sign. I also avoided a common trap: with 37% of players leaving, a model that predicts "nobody leaves" is 63% accurate while catching no one, so accuracy alone would have looked good and meant nothing. The model only uses information from the earlier edition; the later edition only says who left.

Why it's archived
