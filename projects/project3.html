FIFA Player Churn: End-to-End Pipeline + Prediction Model

Football clubs lose players the way subscription businesses lose customers. Two seasons of FIFA rosters, one pipeline, and a model that predicts who leaves next.

DuckDBversioned SQLwindow functions scikit-learnPlotly.js

The story

Using two seasons of FIFA game data (~18K players each), this pipeline takes genuinely raw CSVs (wages as "€565K" strings, duplicate IDs, club names that drift between editions) through ingestion into DuckDB, logged Python cleaning, and versioned SQL transformation into a churn model. The headline data-quality catch: club renames ("Real Madrid CF" → "Real Madrid") masquerade as entire squads transferring at once. A majority-vote entity resolution written in SQL caught 92 renames and removed 8.4 points of phantom churn. One command reproduces everything from raw data to trained models.

Two models are compared honestly: gradient boosting wins ranking power (AUC 0.690), the logistic regression supplies the why, and with 36.8% churn, the docs explain explicitly why accuracy alone would be a lie ("nobody leaves" scores 63% while catching nothing). The live dashboard shows every player's out-of-fold predicted risk next to what actually happened, searchable by club, honest by design.

Key decisions I'd defend in an interview

  • DuckDB over a SQL server: full analytical SQL, embedded, one-command reproducible; the SQL ports to Snowflake/BigQuery nearly unchanged.
  • Leakage rule: features come only from the earlier season's snapshot; the later season contributes exactly one thing: the label.
  • Honest ceiling: AUC 0.69 is what this data supports; the missing feature is contract expiry, and claiming more would be a red flag.
  • Star drain finding: top-3-rated players churn more than squad fillers; retention risk concentrates on your best assets.
